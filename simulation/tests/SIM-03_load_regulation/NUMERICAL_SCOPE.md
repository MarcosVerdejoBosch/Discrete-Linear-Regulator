# SIM-03: justification of the 2 us maximum time step

The accepted purpose is nominal, settled load regulation at an 8 V source and 27 C. The main sequence holds each changed load for about 40 ms and averages the final 10 ms. Load transitions last 100 us. SIM-06 separately characterizes fast load-step peaks and settling.

The 2 us setting is a maximum transient step, not a fixed sampling period. LTspice retains smaller adaptive steps where needed; waveform compression is disabled. Inspection of the completed RAW files gives:

| Recorded-data check | 5 V | 3.3 V |
|---|---:|---:|
| Minimum samples in a final 10 ms window | 14,619 | 14,455 |
| Largest saved interval in those windows | 2 us | 2 us |
| Maximum change of mean using final 2 / 5 / 10 ms | 0.367 uV | 0.289 uV |
| Difference after returning to the initial 1 A load | 0.258 uV | 0.091 uV |

The regulator remains enabled at all evaluated loads. These checks support using the present configuration for the stated settled-mean comparison, with the precision and nominal-model limitations shown in the report. They do **not** establish independence from integration settings, absolute sub-microvolt accuracy, or tolerance/temperature performance. In particular, the very small 3.3 V endpoint difference must not be interpreted as a precisely resolved nonzero slope; the report uses a coarse bound and shows the intermediate load points.

The additional 1 us runs did not complete during startup. They remain recorded as unsuccessful optional numerical checks, not failed load-regulation results and not completed accuracy evidence. They are not required to reproduce the accepted primary scenario. No resistor value, model parameter or parasitic was changed to make the nominal case pass.

## Scope decisions

- Retain the current 2 us maximum-step SIM-03 sources and the accepted solver settings.
- Keep the failed finer-step attempts in the diagnostic history. Stronger numerical-accuracy claims would require a successful independent convergence study.
- A tolerance study, if later needed, must state which component varies and its actual tolerance, examine more than the value that happens to converge, and compare resulting regulation and protection behavior. Real component tolerances alone do not explain a numerical failure.
- The accepted adjusted-threshold SIM-04 protocol is 8 to 4 V, covering output-loss and UVLO events. The former 2.8 V tail is outside that scope. Its failure neither invalidates the separately completed 8-to-4 V tests nor validates behavior at 2.8 V, complete power loss or subsequent recovery.
